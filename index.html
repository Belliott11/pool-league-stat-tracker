Game Stats

Season Rates (Individual)

Every counting stat here (PTS, OREB, DREB, AST, STL, BLK, TOV, PF, Pts Allowed, Beaten, Stops, Def Impact) is a rate per 20 combined points scored in the game, not a season total or a per-game average — only games with real shots logged count toward these at all, so a game that's just been rostered but not reviewed yet doesn't drag anyone's numbers down. Games are capped at different totals (16 or 21), so a plain per-game average isn't a fair comparison between a player who mostly plays 16-point games and one who mostly plays 21s — the game's combined final score stands in for "how much game happened," since possessions aren't tracked. Includes shooting splits and defensive/advanced numbers derived from each game's Shot Log — see the Game Stats table in Stat Entry for what each one means. Def Impact is Stops minus Beaten minus 0.4×Pts Allowed — 0 for anyone never tagged as a defender, not a penalty for conservative tagging. Two-Way is GmSc plus Def Impact. Click a player's name for their full game log and head-to-head history. Click a column header to sort by it (click again to flip direction).

Awards vs. Stats

Summer 2026's voted awards, from that season's closed ballot, checked against the closest tracked stat — computed live from whatever games are actually logged in this browser, so it's only as complete as your Shot Log is. A stat winner sitting outside the top of its column isn't a bug — the vote and the numbers are allowed to disagree, that's the point of comparing them.

Power Ranking vs. Performance

A "power ranking" isn't voted or tracked by this tool — it's the real site's own per-night rank/percentile for each party (rank 1 = best that night, percentile field-size-normalized: 100 = first, 0 = last), pulled from the season spreadsheet as a frozen historical record. Paired here with that same player's actual Two-Way/20 in just the games logged for that date, computed live. Only nights with at least one reviewed game show up here at all — the other 10 real parties predate any footage existing, and a night with footage nobody's gotten to reviewing yet is left out too, rather than showing a table of nothing but dashes. A player with no games logged for that specific night still shows "—", not a zero.

Two-Way Quadrant

GmSc/20 (offense) vs. Def Impact/20 for every player who's played, plotted separately instead of pre-summed into Two-Way Score — so "who's good" splits into "good at what." Quadrant lines are drawn at zero on each axis.

Volume vs. Efficiency

Offense only, separate from the Two-Way Quadrant above — pure shot volume (FGA/20) on the x-axis against season TS% on the y-axis, so a high-volume/low-efficiency player and a low-volume/high-efficiency player show up as mirror opposites directly, instead of needing someone to cross-reference the FGA and TS% columns on the main table by hand.

League Shot Heatmap

Every marked field goal, league-wide, bucketed by court zone — color is FG% in that zone (red low, green high), the number is attempts. A zone stays blank until at least one shot lands in it. Only shots with a marked location are included (see Export → Backfill Shot Locations to fill in the rest).

Head-to-Head Matchup Grid

Every scorer down one axis, every defender across the other — the league-wide extension of the per-player Head-to-Head tables on Player Detail, which only ever show one player's matchups at a time. Each cell is that scorer's FG% against that specific defender (color follows the same red-low/green-high scale as the heatmap above, opacity ramping up with sample size). A blank cell means that pairing has never been tagged. Rows and columns are sorted by attempts, most data first. Not filtered to field goals only — same behavior as the Head-to-Head tables it extends.

Wide-Open Shooting

Every field goal attempt with no tagged defender at all — the opposite end of the Matchup Grid above. Free throws are excluded entirely (they're uncontested by rule, not by circumstance, so folding them in would trivially inflate the numbers). Share of FGA is how much of a player's own shot diet was untagged, for context on how much signal the TS% column actually carries. Click any column header to sort.

Assist Connections

Every passer-to-scorer connection, league-wide — who set up whom, and how often. Directional: Alice assisting Bob is a separate row from Bob assisting Alice, since those are different facts. Sorted by assist count. Only connections with at least one assist are listed.

Teammate Lift Matrix

The pairwise With/Without comparison the Best Teammate award's Average Teammate Lift boils into one number per player — here as a full grid instead: row player on the team, column player's own Two-Way/20 change as a result (green = boosts them, red = drags them down; opacity tracks how large the swing is relative to the biggest one on the grid). A blank cell means that pairing hasn't logged games both with and without each other yet. This grid is not symmetric — row A / column B ("does A help B") and row B / column A ("does B help A") are two different facts about two different people's games, not mirror images of each other.

Shot Distance

Every field goal splits further by how far it actually was from the hoop, on both sides of the 3pt line — Close and Midrange split the 2PT zone at its midpoint; Line (a normal, makeable three right at the line — Poolean's three is straight, not a curved arc, hence "Line" not "Arc") and Deep (a much lower-percentage near-pool-length heave) split the 3PT zone the same way the plain 3PT% on the table above blends together. Both breakpoints are radial distance from the hoop against a single adjustable constant (`CLOSE_RANGE_THRESHOLD` and `THREE_PT_DEEP_THRESHOLD` in app.js) — the 3PT one drawn from an early season sample, the 2PT one a rougher starting guess with no shot volume behind it yet, so treat it as even more provisional. Each zone column shows FG% there (click to sort); the small percentage beneath it is that zone's share of the player's own attempts — how well they shoot from there, and how much they lean on it, in one cell instead of two separate panels. The Mix column is the same shot-selection breakdown as a quick visual (color tracks typical shot quality: green close, amber midrange, teal at the line, red deep). Only field goals with a marked shot location count (see Export → Backfill Shot Locations to fill in the rest). Treat any one player's split as an early read, not a verdict, until more games are logged.

League TS% Over Time

True Shooting % pooled across every player, per date with a reviewed game — a single league-wide efficiency line, useful for spotting drift over the season or checking the effect of a future rule change. Not a player-comparison stat.

TS% by Shot Distance

True Shooting %, league-wide, for each of the four Shot Distance bands — the same numbers as the Shot Distance table above, laid out so "shooting gets worse with distance" (or wherever it actually breaks down) lands in one glance instead of a table someone has to compare in their head. Free throws have no shot location, so this excludes them (unlike the over-time chart above) — each bar is just that zone's own points-per-attempt. Only field goals with a marked shot location count.

Out-of-Bounds Misses

How often a player's own missed shot ends up out of bounds — per Poolean's rule, whoever last touched the ball loses possession, so this is really a "how often does the ball leave their hands for good on a miss" read, not a shooting-accuracy stat. Only misses count as the denominator (a make can't go out of bounds). Sorted by number of misses.

Second-Chance Conversion

"True" second-chance conversion, not just who grabs offensive rebounds — an OREB counts as converted when, within 20 seconds of the missed shot's own video timestamp, either that rebounder scored themselves or someone else scored with them credited as the assist (either path counts, a kick-out is worth the same as a putback). Both the miss and the scoring follow-up need a real video timestamp to be checked; an OREB logged without one still counts toward the total but can't be evaluated for conversion. The 20-second window is a single adjustable constant (`SECOND_CHANCE_WINDOW_SECONDS` in app.js), same pattern as the shot-distance thresholds. This panel runs the same algorithm as scripts/second-chance-analysis.js, just against whatever's loaded in this browser instead of an exported file.

Game-Winning Buckets

How many times a player's shot was the one that actually closed out a game their team won — the real game-ending basket in a race-to-a-target format. Worth being precise about what this is: in a race-to-target format with no clock, the last basket of every decided game is *by definition* scored by the winning team — so this isn't tracking rare, buzzer-beater moments, it's a "who tends to be the one closing games out" tally that grows by exactly one per fully-timestamped decided game. That's exactly why the Clutch comparison above is built on Close-Game Shooting (below) instead of this — still a real signal here (it correlates with who's on the floor and scoring late in wins), just not a scarce one. Only credited when that specific shot has a real video timestamp; without one, there's no trustworthy way to say it was really the last shot of the game, so the game is skipped rather than guessed at. A season count, not a rate.

Close-Game Shooting

This is the tracked stat behind the Clutch comparison above — a margin-aware read, not Game-Winning Buckets below, since GWB is explicitly non-scarce by construction (the last basket of every decided game belongs to the winning team, full stop) and measures "who tends to close games out," not performance under real pressure. This instead is TS%, but only counting attempts from games that actually finished close: decided by 5 points or fewer (`CLUTCH_MARGIN_THRESHOLD` in app.js, a starting guess, not a value backed by real season margin data yet). Tied games count here even though a tie has no "winning shot" for GWB to credit. Click any column header to sort.

Best & Worst Individual Games

Every player-game ranked by that single game's actual Two-Way score (Game Score + Defensive Impact) — deliberately not a per-20 rate or a season total, since the rest of this page normalizes for fair player-to-player comparison and that averages away exactly the thing this panel exists to surface: one specific night's story, buried otherwise inside that game's own box score. Every player on either roster for a reviewed game gets a row, even a quiet one. On a thin season the two lists are capped so they never show the same handful of games twice, just reversed.

Two-Way Trend

Two-Way/20 for every reviewed game this player's played, in order — the line-graph version of the "Last 5" number and arrow already shown elsewhere (feeding the Most Improved comparison on the Leaderboard), since a real trend line makes "up or down lately, and by how much" legible at a glance instead of two numbers to compare in your head. The dashed line is the season average — where the recent points sit relative to it is the whole read.

Shot Chart

Every marked field goal this player has taken, plotted at its actual spot on the court — green for a make, red for a miss. The individual-shot pattern the zone-bucketed heatmap below necessarily smooths over: a cluster, a gap, a lean to one side, is visible here in a way no table of percentages shows directly. Only shots with a marked location are included (see Export → Backfill Shot Locations to fill in the rest).

Shot Heatmap

Every marked field goal this player has taken, bucketed by court zone — color is FG% in that zone (red low, green high), the number is attempts. Only shots with a marked location are included.

Defensive Heatmap

Every shot this player was tagged defending, bucketed by court zone — color is opponent FG% allowed there (red high, green low — inverted from the offensive heatmap above, since a low percentage is good defense). This is what actually answers whether this player's overall Opp FG% holds up at every distance or collapses somewhere specific — a single season number can't say that on its own. A double-teamed shot counts toward every tagged defender, same rule the Head-to-Head — As Defender table below uses.
